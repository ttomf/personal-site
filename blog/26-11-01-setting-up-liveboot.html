Setting up liveboot

2026-11-01

As another reward from Hack Club, I chose the Storage Grant. I used it to buy a 256 GB NVMe SSD (about 237 GiB) and an NVMe enclosure, which lets me connect the drive to my PC via USB, just like a regular external SSD.

Why an NVMe SSD with an enclosure

For a portable system, performance is important. A regular USB flash drive simply cannot match the speed of an NVMe SSD. NVMe SSDs also generally offer better endurance and are less likely to wear out after repeated writes. The enclosure is also reusable, so swapping the drive for another NVMe SSD is straightforward.

Preparing

I wanted both fast, convenient storage and a portable system, so I split the drive into three partitions:

  1. 150 GiB exFAT for general-purpose storage
  2. 1 GiB FAT32 (EFI System Partition) for bootloader files
  3. 86 GiB ext4 for Arch Linux

The exFAT partition has to come first because some devices cannot access partitions other than the first one.

I used standard tools: cfdisk, mkfs.exfat, mkfs.fat -F 32 and mkfs.ext4.

Installing Arch Linux

The installation itself was nothing new; I've installed Arch Linux many times before. However, this time I had to make sure that the bootloader was installed to the external drive's EFI System Partition without creating a boot entry in the PC's UEFI NVRAM. This was achieved using the --removable flag in grub-install.

For better portability, I also had to remove the autodetect hook from mkinitcpio.conf. Otherwise, the initramfs would only include necessary modules for the hardware detected on my PC, causing problems on different machines.

Final result

Now I have a portable Arch Linux installation alongside 150 GiB of general-purpose storage, all on a single external NVMe drive. Instead of carrying my laptop everywhere, I can simply plug the drive into another computer and boot from it.
